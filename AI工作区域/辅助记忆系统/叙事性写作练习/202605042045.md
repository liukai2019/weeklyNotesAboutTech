### 1. New Concept English (Book 4)
**Lesson 1: Finding fossil man** (by Robin Place)

**Complete Original Text:**  
We can read of things that happened 5,000 years ago in the Near East, where people first learned to write. But there are some parts of the world where even now people cannot write. The only way that they can preserve their history is to recount it as sagas -- legends handed down from one generation of storytellers to another. These legends are useful because they can tell us something about migrations of people who lived long ago, but none could write down what they did. Anthropologists wondered where the remote ancestors of the Polynesian peoples now living in the Pacific Islands came from. The sagas of these people explain that some of them came from Indonesia about 2,000 years ago.  
But the first people who were like ourselves lived so long ago that even their sagas, if they had any, are forgotten. So archaeologists have neither history nor legends to help them to find out where the first 'modern men' came from.  

Fortunately, however, ancient men made tools of stone, especially flint, because this is easier to shape than other kinds. They may also have used wood and skins, but these have rotted away. Stone does not decay, and so the tools of long ago have remained when even the bones of the men who made them have disappeared without trace.

**中文参考译文：**  
我们从书籍中可读到5,000年前近东发生的事情，那里的人最早学会了写字。但直到现在，世界上有些地方，人们还不会书写。他们保存历史的唯一办法是将历史当作传说讲述，由讲述人一代接一代地将史实描述为传奇故事口传下来。这些传说是有用的，因为它们能告诉我们远古人类迁移的一些情况，但是没有人能把他们所做的事情记载下来。人类学家过去不清楚如今生活在太平洋诸岛上的波利尼西亚人的祖先来自何方，当地人的传说却告诉人们：其中一部分是约在2,000年前从印度尼西亚迁来的。  

但是，和我们相似的原始人生活的年代太久远了，因此，有关他们的传说即使有如今也失传了。于是，考古学家们既缺乏历史记载，又无口头传说来帮助他们弄清最早的“现代人”是从哪里来的。  

然而，幸运的是，远古人用石头制作了工具，特别是用燧石，因为燧石较之其他石头更容易成形。他们也可能用过木头和兽皮，但这类东西早已腐烂殆尽。石头是不会腐烂的。因此，尽管制造这些工具的人的骨头早已荡然无存，但远古时代的石头工具却保存了下来。

**重点词汇/短语（6个，带例句）：**  
1. **Recount** (v.): To narrate or tell in detail. *She recounted the events of the trip with great enthusiasm.*  
2. **Saga** (n.): A long heroic story or legend. *The family saga spanned several generations of immigrants.*  
3. **Migration** (n.): Movement from one place to another. *The migration of birds happens every autumn.*  
4. **Anthropologist** (n.): A scientist who studies human societies and cultures. *The anthropologist lived with the tribe for two years.*  
5. **Archaeologist** (n.): A scientist who studies ancient remains. *Archaeologists discovered ancient ruins in the desert.*  
6. **Rot away / Decay** (v.): To decompose or break down. *The wooden ship had rotted away after centuries underwater.*  
7. **Without trace**: Completely disappeared. *The missing files vanished without trace.*  
8. **Hand down**: Pass on to the next generation. *These traditions are handed down from father to son.*

**写作模仿建议：**  
Imitate the clear, logical explanatory style that contrasts written history with oral traditions and archaeological evidence. Try writing a short paragraph (150-200 words) on a similar theme: "How do we know about ancient civilizations without written records?" Use contrasts (e.g., "But..."), explanatory connectors ("fortunately, however"), and precise vocabulary about preservation and evidence. Focus on building a logical flow from problem to solution.

**更自然的英文表达：**
We can read about things that happened 5,000 years ago in the Near East, where people were the first to learn to write. But even today, there are some parts of the world where people still cannot write. The only way they can preserve their history is to recount it as sagas—legends handed down from one generation to the next. These sagas are useful because they can tell us about the migrations of people who lived long ago, but none could write down what they did. Archaeologists did not know where the remote ancestors of the Polynesian peoples now living in the Pacific Islands came from. However, the sagas of these people explain that some of them came from Indonesia about 2,000 years ago.
